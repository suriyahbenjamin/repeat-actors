About this data

The cases here come from the client database of the Innocence Project Data & Research Center, where I worked as an intern. My assignment was an audit of that database: checking what had already been entered, tracking down what was missing, and getting every case into a consistent format. Prosecutor names turned out to be the largest gap. They had been recorded unevenly across the file, or not at all, so most of that column was built during the audit.

A note on totals. Some cases were tried by more than one prosecutor. Each named prosecutor is credited with the full duration of the case, so the person-year figures for individual prosecutors add up to more than the total for the file as a whole.

Each case was rebuilt from primary documents. Sources included trial transcripts, charging documents and indictments, police and investigative reports, docket sheets, appellate briefs and opinions, post-conviction and habeas filings, court orders, and orders of exoneration or dismissal, supplemented by contemporaneous news coverage and public exoneration registries where the record was incomplete. Prosecutors were identified from the appearance of counsel in trial transcripts and from signature blocks on filings, rather than from secondary summaries.
